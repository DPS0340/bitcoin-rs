# Offline full-validation comparison

## Status and evidence boundary

Issue #34 asked for a symmetric offline comparison in which bitcoin-rs and Bitcoin Core build chainstate from the same block payloads while doing matched validation, persistence, cache, index, and correctness work. The strict method now lives in `tools/benchmark-campaign/runner.py` and `tools/benchmark-campaign/native_offline.py`.

There are two evidence layers, and they must not be conflated:

1. [`bounded-performance-custody-v1.json`](data/end-to-end-sync/bounded-performance-custody-v1.json) records an executed mainnet 0–150,000 comparison. The published medians are 39.250889507 seconds for bitcoin-rs replay and 64.9216206073761 seconds for Bitcoin Core chainstate reindex, a Core/bitcoin-rs ratio of 1.6540165439001833. The twofold target was not met. This receipt predates the current `benchmark-campaign-result-v2` controller and contains three runs per role, not seven controller pairs.
2. The current controller makes the #34 method executable and fail-closed. `test_complete_seven_pair_run_and_round_trip` exercises the full synthetic controller path in the repository test suite. It is not a live C150 or Cmodern performance run.

No `benchmark-campaign-result-v2` receipt for a real C150 or Cmodern cell is committed here. This page therefore closes the comparator-method question and preserves the bounded result already published in [End-to-end sync benchmarks](end-to-end-sync.md). It does not claim that the current seven-pair controller has completed a live corpus campaign.

## The comparison contract

A ready offline cell binds one `bitcoin_rs_replay_v3` candidate to one `bitcoin_core_loadblock_v31` reference. Both arms inherit the same read-only corpus descriptor from `_snapshot_inputs`; the candidate also receives the manifest descriptor. `_verify_command_semantics` admits only these workloads:

- bitcoin-rs: the exact required option set, `--stop-height` at the certified height, `--blocks-file` bound to the inherited corpus descriptor, `--corpus-manifest`, `--dbcache-mb` equal to the cell's shared `cache_budget_mb`, `--assume-valid-height 0`, `--storage-backend` fixed to the cell backend, and `--data-dir`/`--output` under the fresh arm directory;
- Bitcoin Core 31.1: the exact required argument set, `-loadblock` bound to the same corpus descriptor, `-assumevalid=0`, `-blocksxor=0`, `-dbcache` equal to the same shared `cache_budget_mb`, `-stopatheight` at the certified height, `-connect=0`, `-listen=0`, `-dnsseed=0`, `-fixedseeds=0`, `-disablewallet=1`, `-server=1`, and `-datadir`/`-debuglogfile` under the fresh arm directory.

The comparison is chainstate construction, not file parsing alone. Before timing, `parse_cell_proof` requires both roles to certify the same `CertifiedState`: height, best block, UTXO count, total amount in satoshis, MuHash, and bitcoin-rs's serialized-UTXO checksum. It also requires successful durability evidence for both roles and at least one Bitcoin Core restart. The role/cell/product proof scope prevents evidence from one role, backend, architecture, or corpus cell from certifying another.

The controller then executes exactly seven pairs. `schedule_for` chooses the first arm in each pair from the cell key and schedule seed, with each role first three or four times. `run_cell` completes both arms of a pair before starting the next pair. Every arm gets unique output, result, log, and data paths. `classify_wall_performance` takes the median of seven positive whole-process wall measurements for each role and computes `core_median / candidate_median`.

Correctness wins over speed. `_derive` can compute medians only after all seven pairs are valid, but its verdict precedence is `fail_correctness`, `invalid_env`, `fail_run`, then the performance verdict. A fast arm with a wrong tip, missing full-validation witness, invalid environment, failed process, incomplete measurement, or mismatched peer state cannot pass.

## What the bounded receipt establishes

The committed bounded receipt supports these historical facts and no stronger claim:

- both roles processed mainnet genesis through height 150,000 and reported stop hash `0000000000000a3290f20e75860d505ce0e948a1d1d846bec7e39015d242884b`;
- both disabled assume-valid and txindex (`corpus.bitcoin_core.txindex` and `corpus.bitcoin_rs.txindex` are false), and Bitcoin Core used a 450 MiB dbcache;
- bitcoin-rs used a fresh output directory for each of three runs, and Core used three restores;
- the median replay/reindex values were 39.250889507 and 64.9216206073761 seconds respectively;
- the measured 1.6540165439001833 ratio did not meet the 2.0 target;
- the receipt binds the Core binary, source commit, corpus containers, manifests, resource guards, and omitted raw result files to SHA-256 values to the extent shown in that schema.

It does not retrospectively become a seven-pair `benchmark-campaign-result-v2` run. It also does not prove byte-for-byte payload equality between its distinct Core and bitcoin-rs corpus containers. Those limits are part of the record, not reasons to discard the measurement.

## Corpus custody dependency (#42)

Issue #34 owns the comparison method. Issue #42 owns the durable corpus and state commitments beneath campaign cells and remains open. The custody-mechanism items it listed as missing from the tree are now supplied in-tree:

- a deterministic, versioned exporter (`export_active_chain_corpus` and `export_corpus_from_rest` in `crates/node/src/corpus.rs`) that streams genesis through the stop height into Core-framed `-loadblock` archives and publishes the archive before the validated manifest;
- a versioned per-height payload/hash manifest (`bitcoin-rs-corpus-manifest` v2) pinning offset, length, and block hash for the exact bytes both arms inherit;
- frozen product identities for exactly C150 and Cmodern (corpus id, stop height, stop hash), enforced at construction, structural validation, and wire ingestion;
- `ExportCustody` binding the exporting Core version plus one reopen proof per supported backend, and replay preflight rejecting manifests whose network, genesis, range, or archive size contradict the invocation;
- controller-side hash binding: a ready cell admits only a corpus, manifest, and role/cell/product proof whose digests the proof and result contracts re-verify.

What still does not exist — and what this page does not claim: a live seven-pair `benchmark-campaign-result-v2` receipt for any real cell, a committed frozen C150 or Cmodern archive, a raw census bound to those bytes, raw Core oracle transcripts, or recorded all-backend reopen evidence. C150 remains a historical candidate identity and Cmodern an unresolved selection until an operator runs the exporter and freezes those artifacts. The comparator can reject missing or mismatched custody, but it cannot manufacture evidence.
